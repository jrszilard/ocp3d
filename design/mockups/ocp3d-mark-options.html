OCP3D · mark study, rounds 2–3 · 2026-09-28 · local board, not production art

Print it. Measure it.

Founder feedback on round 1: keep the measurement line, but the layers weren't obvious. Stacked bars alone read as pancakes, books or a pyramid. What does say "3D printed" is a nozzle laying the top layer. Every option keeps the one-colour stamp ring and is drawn from 16 px outward. None shows a single part, a car silhouette or a shield.

CHOSEN · ROUND 3

The plate

Founder: "add something simple that evokes an old car". The lockup becomes an embossed licence plate: OCP3D reads like a vanity plate, and OLD CAR PROBLEMS sits where a plate's slogan goes. A generic plate, with no state name, format or motto.

The mark stays a method symbol. The plate is the one car cue; the stamp alone serves the favicon and avatar.

CHOSEN · ROUND 2

F3 · Print it, measure it

A nozzle lays the top layer of a part, and the part's width is dimensioned underneath, the way it would be on a technical drawing. It reads top to bottom as the whole job: print it, then check it measures right.

The rounded layers look like extruded plastic, and the nozzle makes "3D printed" unmistakable from 24 px up.

E2 · Measure over the print

Round 1's layout, with a nozzle added to the top layer. It keeps the measurement line on top.

The nozzle hangs off the right-hand arrow, so it reads as three separate symbols rather than one scene.

D5 · Measurement only

Just the measurement line, bold. The strongest at 16 px, and "3D" in the name carries the printing.

On its own it can pass for a generic "width" or "expand" icon.
