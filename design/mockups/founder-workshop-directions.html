Design brief / 2026.09

The look of a one-car workshop becoming a useful catalog.

The new offer is not “a 3D-printing marketplace.” It is a founder solving real parts problems on his own 1983 A60, preserving the evidence, then helping the next owner. These are three deliberately different visual languages for that same honest promise. All use genuine bench imagery; none implies a fit, sale, or vehicle result that has not been earned.
PERSONALOne real owner and one real workbench—not anonymous platform language.
PRACTICALRequesting a part should feel like talking to a capable specialist.
EVIDENCEDMeasured, fitted, and requested remain distinct, literal states.
ORIGINALNo copied OEM manual, aftermarket catalog, or franchise trade dress.
Direction 01 / tactile founder storyBench
Journal
A warm, imperfect field notebook from a real garage. Real parts, tape, graph paper, process notes, and a human first-person voice make the origin feel tangible. The evidence system is still there, but it feels earned rather than institutional.
Best forTrust, warmth, personal connection, donor requests.

Watch forToo crafty or scrapbook-like if the measurement content loses precision.

Direction 02 / practical specialistIndependent
Parts Counter
A niche supplier with a rigorous back room. This puts the part and its current state first: clear inventory language, high contrast, strong product labels, and a direct route to ask for help. It feels ready to become commerce without pretending the catalog is already stocked.
Best forConversion, clarity, future repair kits and made-to-order sales.

Watch forIt can feel more like a store than the founder story, or overstate availability if the states soften.
